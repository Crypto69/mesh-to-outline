Mesh to Outline

for Autodesk® Fusion® · version {{VERSION}}

Mesh to Outline cuts a mesh body with a plane and draws the section as a normal sketch of lines, arcs, circles and fitted splines, ready to extrude, loft or dimension. It can also draw a whole stack of sections between two picks and extrude them into one solid body, or loft one body through them along two rails.

It runs only on your computer. It collects no data and needs no internet connection. Privacy policy.

Requirements

  • The current release of Autodesk Fusion, on Windows 64-bit or macOS.
  • Nothing else: everything the add-in needs is inside it.

Where to find it

Mesh tab → Modify panel → Mesh to Outline, next to Convert Mesh. If Fusion's Mesh panel is not found, the button is under Utilities → Add-Ins instead; the Text Commands palette says where.

To turn the add-in off or on: Utilities → Add-Ins → Scripts and Add-Ins → Add-Ins tab → MeshSectionSketch → Stop / Run.

Quick start

  1. Insert → Insert Mesh, and set the units to mm.
  2. For one section or a stack, make a construction plane where you want the cut (Construct → Offset Plane), or use any flat face.
  3. Mesh tab → Modify → Mesh to Outline.
  4. Choose the Action first: Single slice, Stack or X-Ray. Pick the mesh body, then the plane (Single slice, Stack) or the start and end (X-Ray), and click OK.

Tidy corners, rails and loft

Tidy corners is for parts made of flats and rounds. Without it, each curve is fitted on its own, so a fillet can meet its wall at a slight angle or a hair apart. With it, the curves of a loop are chained through shared sketch points and each fillet is tangent to its walls, so the sketch behaves like one you drew. Curves only move where they still fit the mesh within the Fit tolerance; anywhere they would not, the plain fit is kept. On organic shapes it changes little.

Rails are two extra sections at 90° to the slices, along the stack. Where a rail crosses a slice it is pinned onto that slice's outline, which is what Fusion's Loft needs. A rail that misses a slice, crosses it twice, or runs along an inner wall is still drawn, but it is not used, and the closing message says which slices it missed.

Loft joins the largest outline of each slice into one new body, guided by every rail that touches all the slices. If Fusion refuses the rails it lofts without them; if it refuses that too, the sketches and rails are kept. Fusion's Loft uses each slice's outer outline only and fills the holes (it shows their outlines in red); with Cut holes into the loft each hole that runs on through two or more slices is cut back out with a loft of its own. A loft works best when the outline keeps the same shape from slice to slice. Where parts appear and disappear along the stack, use Extrude each slice instead.

Undo and uninstall

Each run adds sketches (and, if ticked, one body) to your design; Undo or deleting them removes them. To uninstall, use the Autodesk Marketplace uninstaller (Windows: Apps & features; macOS: remove the MeshToOutline.bundle folder from ~/Library/Application Support/Autodesk/ApplicationPlugins).
